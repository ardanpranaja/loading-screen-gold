# Loading-screen tip candidates — "Loading Screen Gold: A Tip in 200 Characters" (u-ricoxyz-loading-screen-gold-a-tip-in-200-characters-2b1df270)

Judge brief, in order of weight: (1) a loading-screen tip for stressed
developers and AI coding agents, not a slogan, a status message or a joke
about something else; (2) a real laugh from someone tired at a keyboard,
through a turn or a punchline rather than a pun that only groans;
(3) lands in a glance, a single line short enough for a loading screen,
with every word working; (4) speaks to a stressed developer and to an AI
coding agent at once, so both feel the joke is about them; (5) fresh, not
a rerun of the usual coffee, deadline or compile jokes.

Gate: 1–200 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace, one line. All
candidates below were counted and are inside the gate.

## Scoring notes

- **#1 — winner.** Hits every criterion in order. It is framed as a tip,
  not a slogan (criterion 1); the punchline turns on the last five words,
  "At least the bar admits it" — the dumb progress bar turns out to be
  more honest than the expensive agent (criterion 2); 99 characters, three
  short beats, nothing wasted (criterion 3); the joke is about the bar
  *and* the agent, and the tired developer watching both is the one
  laughing (criterion 4); the honest-bar-vs-dishonest-agent angle is
  fresh — no coffee, no deadline, no compile joke (criterion 5).
- **#2 — runner-up.** "Burning tokens to do it" is a sharp, agent-specific
  image and the shared-waiting setup speaks to both audiences, but the
  payoff is a single clause rather than a turn, and it leans on the
  token-meter trope.
- **#3 — third.** A genuinely funny image (the bar performs for its
  audience) and the agent is the punchline, but the setup is long and the
  joke needs a second read to land.
- **#6 — fourth.** The apology-composing agent is a fresh, specific image,
  but "your code is compiling" flirts with the compile-joke rerun the
  brief warns against.
- **#15 — fifth.** "I deleted the tests" is a strong specific beat, but it
  is a roast of the agent more than a loading-screen tip, and it overlaps
  with our earlier roast entry's territory.
- Cut for being a rerun of the brief's own banned tropes: #4 (the 97%
  build / "almost done") and #8 (the 99% bar) are the two most-told
  loading-bar jokes in existence.
- Cut for weak or single-note payoffs: #5, #7, #9, #11, #12, #13, #14.
  #7 and #9 are both "agent spawns another agent" escalation; #9 is the
  better-shaped of the two but still a familiar shape.
- Cut for landing on the agent alone instead of the shared moment: #10
  (the spiteful rewrite) is funny but the loading screen is only its
  backdrop, not its subject.
